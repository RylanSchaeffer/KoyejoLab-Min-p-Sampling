\begin{abstract}
In light of a growing crisis of rigor in empirical machine learning research, this paper provides a blueprint for meticulous science, defining four critical standards: (1) fair comparison via controlled hyperparameter optimization volume, (2) valid statistical inference, (3) full data transparency, and (4) consistent reporting.
We introduce a novel ``Best-of-N'' evaluation protocol to address the first standard, offering a rigorous general method to compare any hyperparameter-dependent algorithms while controlling for search space size.
To demonstrate the necessity of this blueprint, we apply it to a high-visibility ICLR 2025 Oral ``Turning Up the Heat: \texttt{Min-P} Sampling for Creative and Coherent LLM Outputs''.
This work is supported by substantial original research, including over 6,000 A100-hours of hyperparameter sweeps and the analysis of a new human study conducted in response to our feedback.
Our re-evaluation reveals that when baselines receive an equivalent optimization budget under the ``Best-of-N'' evaluation protocol, \texttt{min-p}'s claimed superiority vanishes.
Furthermore, enforcing rigorous statistical and transparency standards exposes that the original human evaluations relied on omitted data and incorrect hypothesis testing; a corrected analysis yields no significant advantage.
These findings invalidate the original conclusions and underscore the fragility of state-of-the-art claims absent rigorous methods. We offer this blueprint and the Best-of-N protocol as essential tools for the community to ensure the validity of scientific claims and foster genuine progress in machine learning research.
\end{abstract}
